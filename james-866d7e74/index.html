The whole review in one minute

If you read nothing else, read this.

Where we are

  1. 4.4x the engaged views on a typical video (49.8K in 28 days, up from 11.3K).
  2. Four videos past 100K in their first 28 days. None in the 6 months before.
  3. The hits came in summer: 6 of the 7 winners went out in June to August; since September, one.

What the winners share

  1. The idea, not the click: CTR is the same on hits and misses. Reach is what changes.
  2. The whole retirement playbook, for everyone: how to invest, how to withdraw, what you tell every client.
  3. Warnings beat permission: "Don't retire if..." got 2.48x; "Retire NOW if..." got 0.60x.
  4. Dollar amounts are a safe single: 0.71x to 1.08x, never a hit.

What we change

  1. More big playbooks and warnings, with the authority line ("I'm a retirement advisor", "every client").
  2. Fewer dollar-amount and permission titles.
  3. The whiteboard thumbnail for the big ideas.

Part 3 · What They Have in Common

It's the idea, not the click

The same face, the same thumbnail style, the same production on every video. What changed from hit to miss was the idea, and how far YouTube carried it.

a) Clicks vs reach

Packaging gets the click every time. Reach decides the result.

People clicked the misses as often as the hits. The difference is how many people YouTube showed each video to.

5.3% vs 5.4%typical CTR, top 7 vs bottom 7. Every video landed between 4.5% and 6.0%.
but
1.5M vs 386Ktypical impressions, top 7 vs bottom 7

Your packaging opens the door. The idea decides how many people YouTube shows it to.

Across the 24 scored videos, impressions move almost exactly with the score; CTR doesn't move with it at all.

b) The winning idea

The winners are the whole retirement playbook, for everyone

Five of the top 7 answer one question for anyone near retirement, whatever they've saved: how do I actually run my retirement? They also turn the most viewers into subscribers: 3.5 to 7.2 per 1,000 engaged views, against 2.6 for the typical video.

#2 · 2.44xHow To Invest Once You Retire121.4K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 4.5% · 1.8M impressions
#3 · 2.32xI'm A Retirement Advisor: Here's What I Tell Every Client At 62115.7K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.7% · 1.5M impressions
#4 · 2.01xIf You Only Watch One Retirement Video, Make it This100.3K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.2% · 1.3M impressions

Broad, not personal

Nobody needs to check their balance before clicking How To Invest Once You Retire. Everyone near retirement is the audience.

The advisor's view

"I'm a retirement advisor", "every client", "RANKED": you're the expert who has seen it all, handing over the whole picture.

Everything in one video

If You Only Watch One Retirement Video brought the most subscribers per view on the channel: 7.2 per 1,000.

c) Warnings vs permission

"Don't retire if..." beat "Retire now if..." by 4x

Three videos use the same format: a short checklist on whether you should retire. The warning was the biggest video since June. The two permission versions sit in the bottom 7.

#1 · 2.48xDon’t Retire If This Is You (5 Warning Signs)123.6K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.9% · 1.7M impressions
#22 · 0.60xRetire NOW If You Answer "YES" to All 5 Questions30.0K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.8% · 386K impressions
#18 · 0.69x5 Signs You Should Retire Earlier Than You Think19.9K engaged views in 7 days · CTR 5.5% · 262K impressions

Why

A warning speaks to everyone thinking about retiring ("is this me?"). Permission speaks to the smaller group who already feel ready. The three permission titles got the smallest reach on the channel (262K to 386K impressions) and the fewest subscribers: 1.2 per 1,000 on the one with 28 days of data.

d) Dollar amounts

A dollar amount in the title is a safe single, never a hit

Eight titles led with an amount ($1M, $1.44M, $2M, $2.5M, $3M vs $2M, $100K vs $1M vs $10M). All eight landed between 0.71x and 1.08x: none in the top 7, none in the bottom 7.

An amount narrows the audience to people near that number, so YouTube has fewer people to show it to. It's reliable, which is why it's worth keeping now and then.

e) Same thumbnail, different title

Two weeks apart, the same chart: 0.41x, then 1.64x

Both thumbnails use the time-vs-money chart. The title is what changed: a vague warning at 65, then a new idea at 55.

#24 · 0.41xThink Twice Before You Retire at 6520.4K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.1% · 246K impressions
#6 · 1.64xOnce You Understand This, You'll Stop Working Past 5581.7K engaged views in 28 days · CTR 5.9% · 953K impressions

Same picture, 4x the reach once the title promised something new to understand.

f) What changed after summer

Since September, 7 of 11 videos were a dollar amount or a permission title

In June to August it was 4 of 13. That's the same pattern the October health check found: the dip came from the ideas, not the packaging.

Part 4 · Thumbnails

Your thumbnails are consistent, and doing their job

Every thumbnail since June follows one house style, and CTR stays in a tight 4.5% to 6.0% band. The style isn't what holds a video back.

Your face, on one side

Looking at camera, mid-explanation, taking up a third to half of the frame.

2 to 4 words in the brown box

"You're Not Ready.", "Copy This Strategy", "Stop Working". The words add a verdict; they don't repeat the title.

One visual

A chart, a whiteboard, a tier list or a lifestyle scene. Clean, with one idea per thumbnail.

Which styles sat on the winners

Read this as a fit, not a cause

The whiteboard went on the big "here's the whole picture" ideas, so it shares their success. What it shows: the teacher-at-the-board look matches a big playbook idea, and a dollar figure in the thumbnail does what a dollar title does: a steady middle.

Part 5 · What This Points To

Make more of the big playbook ideas

The format works. The next six months are about which ideas we pick.

Do more of
  • The whole playbook, for everyone near retirement: how to invest, how to withdraw, what you tell every client, the full picture in one video.
  • Warnings: "Don't retire if...", the mistakes you see most.
  • The advisor's view in the title: "I'm a retirement advisor", "every client", "RANKED".
  • The whiteboard thumbnail for the big ideas.
  • New versions of the 7 winners for 2027, from a fresh angle.
Do less of
  • Dollar amounts as the main idea ($2M, $3M vs $2M): keep as an occasional safe single.
  • Permission titles ("Retire NOW if...", "You'll probably never spend...").
  • Narrow single topics (tax strategies, Social Security at 62, things to sell) unless they're framed for everyone.
  • Dollar figures in the thumbnail.
